How this Airbnb calculator works

It starts with your nightly rate and how often you're booked. It adds the cleaning fees guests pay. Then it takes out everything a short-term rental really costs. That means your cleaner on every turnover, the Airbnb host fee, supplies, utilities, repairs and the mortgage.

Furniture and setup count as cash you put in, right next to your down payment. That's why a short-term rental can show big cash flow but a so-so return.

Getting the inputs right

Nightly rate and occupancy make or break the deal. Look at similar listings near the property and check their calendars across a full year, not just summer. Plenty of markets run 55 to 70% occupancy.

You pay the utilities on a short-term rental. Power, water, internet and streaming add up. So do supplies.

Check the local rules first. Lots of cities limit or ban short-term rentals, require a permit or charge a lodging tax. A great number means nothing if you can't legally rent it.

What's a good result?

Short-term rentals take more work and more risk than a regular lease. Many investors want a clearly higher cash-on-cash return to make that worth it. Run the same house as a long-term rental in EquityFoundry and compare the two side by side.
