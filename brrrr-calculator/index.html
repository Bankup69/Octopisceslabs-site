How a BRRRR works

You buy a place that needs work. You fix it up and rent it out. Then you refinance based on what it's worth now, not what you paid. If the new value is high enough, the new loan pays off the old one and hands a big chunk of your cash back. You use that cash on the next one.

The number that matters most is cash left in the deal. Get it to zero and you own a rental with none of your money tied up. Go below zero and you walk away with extra cash.

The math

Cash out = new loan − old loan balance − refinance closing costs

Cash left in = down payment + closing + rehab + holding costs − cash out

The new loan is the after-repair value times the lender's loan-to-value, usually 70 to 75%. Holding costs are the mortgage, taxes and insurance you pay while it's being fixed and has no tenant.

Where BRRRR deals go wrong

The appraisal comes in low. Use the middle of your sold comps for the ARV, not the best one. Rehabs run long and over budget, so pad both. And check the cash flow after the refinance. A bigger loan means a bigger payment, and some deals pull all the cash out but lose money every month.

Many lenders also want you to own the place for 6 months before a cash-out refinance. Ask before you buy.
