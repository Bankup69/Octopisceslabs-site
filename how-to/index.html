How to analyze a long-term rental

The full tour: listing link, purchase, rent, loan and expenses, then what every result means, how to find your offer, and how to compare deals.

    Chapters

  1. 01

    Pick your strategy

    Start at the top and pick your plan for the property: a long-term rental, a short-term rental, BRRRR, or a fix and flip. We'll begin with a long-term rental.

  2. 02

    Add the property

    Paste the listing link from Zillow, Redfin or Realtor.com. EquityFoundry picks up the address and keeps the link, so the photos are always one click away. Give it a name you'll recognize.

  3. 03

    Purchase and costs

    Next, the purchase. Enter your offer price, any repairs before you rent it out, and your closing costs. Not sure what a number should be? Click the little i beside any field. It explains what it means, where to find it, and what's typical.

  4. 04

    Units and rent

    Now the rent. Add a row for each unit type. This duplex has two units at twelve fifty a month. Vacancy starts at five percent, a sensible place to begin.

  5. 05

    Financing

    Then the loan: your down payment, interest rate and loan term.

  6. 06

    Expenses

    Add the property taxes and insurance. Maintenance and big future repairs are already set aside as a share of the rent, so they're counted from day one. Using a property manager? Add their fee here.

  7. 07

    Read the results

    Now look to the right. This is your answer. Cash flow is what's left each month after every bill and the mortgage. Cash-on-cash return is what your cash earns each year. Cap rate weighs the property's income against its price, before any loan. DSCR tells a lender whether the income covers the payment. And the one percent rule is a quick gut check.

  8. 08

    Year 1 breakdown

    The year one breakdown shows every dollar, line by line, from gross rent all the way down to cash flow.

  9. 09

    Find your offer

    Numbers too tight? Change the price and watch everything update. That's how you find your offer.

  10. 10

    Pro features

    With Pro, the long-term projection looks thirty years down the road: your equity, your IRR, and what you'd walk away with at the sale. Pro also unlocks short-term rentals, BRRRR and flips, plus a PDF report for your lender.

  11. 11

    Save and compare

    When it looks right, save it. The dashboard keeps every deal side by side. Sort by cash flow and the best one rises to the top.

How to analyze a short-term rental

Nightly rate, occupancy, cleaning fees and the platform's cut. See what an Airbnb-style rental really earns, and test the slow season before you buy.

    Chapters

  1. 01

    Pick short-term rental

    Choose short-term rental at the top. It's part of EquityFoundry Pro.

  2. 02

    Purchase and setup

    Add the listing link, then the price and closing costs. A short-term rental needs furniture and supplies before the first guest arrives, so add your furnishing and setup budget here.

  3. 03

    Nightly income

    Now the income. Enter your nightly rate, how often you expect to be booked, and the average stay. Add the cleaning fee you charge guests, what each cleaning costs you, and the platform's cut. Similar listings nearby will give you honest numbers.

  4. 04

    Loan and expenses

    Then the loan, the taxes and the insurance. You pay the utilities on a short-term rental, so add those too, along with monthly supplies like coffee and linens.

  5. 05

    Read the results

    Here's your answer. Cash flow after every bill and the mortgage. Cash-on-cash return on the money you put in, furniture included. And revenue per night booked, so you can see what each stay is really worth.

  6. 06

    Test the slow season

    Occupancy moves everything. Drop it a few points and watch the cash flow shrink. Test the slow season before it tests you.

  7. 07

    Year 1 breakdown

    The year one breakdown follows every dollar, from bookings and cleaning fees down to what's left.

How to analyze a BRRRR deal

Buy, rehab, rent, refinance, repeat. Follow your cash through the rehab and the refinance, and see how much stays in the deal.

    Chapters

  1. 01

    Pick BRRRR

    Choose BRRRR at the top. It's part of EquityFoundry Pro.

  2. 02

    Purchase and rehab

    Enter the purchase price, your rehab budget, and the closing costs.

  3. 03

    After-repair value and refinance

    Now the part that makes BRRRR work. Enter the after-repair value, what the home will be worth once it's fixed. Add how many months the rehab takes, then the refinance: the loan-to-value your lender allows, the new rate and term, and the closing costs on the new loan.

  4. 04

    Rent and purchase loan

    Add the rent you expect once the work is done, and the loan you'll use to buy it. Rehab loans often carry higher rates, and EquityFoundry counts what you pay while the work gets done.

  5. 05

    Expenses

    Then the taxes and insurance. Maintenance and future big repairs are already set aside.

  6. 06

    Read the results

    Here's your answer. Cash flow after the refinance. Cash left in the deal, what's still tied up once the new loan pays you back. And equity after the refinance, the value you created. The less cash left in, the stronger the BRRRR.

  7. 07

    Follow your cash

    Open buy, rehab and refinance to follow your cash through every stage, from purchase to payoff.

How to analyze a fix and flip

Purchase, rehab, holding costs and the sale. See your projected profit, check the 70% rule, and find the offer that makes the flip worth it.

    Chapters

  1. 01

    Pick fix and flip

    Choose fix and flip at the top. It's part of EquityFoundry Pro.

  2. 02

    Purchase and rehab

    Enter the purchase price, your rehab budget, and the closing costs.

  3. 03

    After-repair value and sale

    Next, the after-repair value: what it should sell for once it's finished. Base it on recent sales of fixed-up homes nearby. Add how many months until it sells, and your selling costs, like agent commissions.

  4. 04

    Loan and holding costs

    Then the loan, plus the taxes, insurance and utilities you pay while you own it. Those holding costs add up, month after month.

  5. 05

    Read the results

    Here's your answer. Projected profit after every cost. ROI on the cash you put in, and annualized ROI, so you can compare a six month flip with a year long one. The seventy percent rule shows the most many flippers would pay: seventy percent of the after-repair value, minus repairs.

  6. 06

    Find your offer

    This price sits above that line. Bring the offer down and watch the profit climb. That's the number to negotiate toward.

  7. 07

    Flip breakdown

    The flip breakdown shows every dollar in and out, from purchase to sale.

How to track rental income and expenses (Ingot)

Add your rentals, log rent and bills with Quick add, split mortgage interest from principal, keep receipts, and get your Schedule E totals ready at tax time.

    Chapters

  1. 01

    Open Ingot

    Tap Ingot in the menu. On a phone, it's called Rentals. Your entries are saved on your device, and if you sign in, they stay in sync across your devices.

  2. 02

    Add a property

    Start on Properties and tap Add property. Give it a name and address, the number of units, the rent you expect each month, and the purchase price and date. Ingot uses the expected rent to show when rent comes in short.

  3. 03

    Log rent

    Quick add is how you log money in or out. There are four kinds. Income, like rent. Expenses, the costs of running the property. Distributions, money you take out, which never lowers your tax numbers. And other, for things like loan principal and deposits you hold. Here's November rent for Unit A: amount, date, property, category, and a note.

  4. 04

    Mortgage payments

    For a mortgage payment, pick mortgage interest. Only the interest is a Schedule E expense, so type the principal part in its own box. Ingot logs it separately as loan principal.

  5. 05

    Receipts

    Repairs work the same way. Pick the property and the category, add a note, and attach a photo of the receipt. On a phone, this opens your camera.

  6. 06

    Dashboard

    The dashboard shows income, expenses, net and distributions for this month, the quarter, the year to date, or the last twelve months. Below that, income against expenses by month, your biggest costs, and how each property compares, including rent collected against what you expected.

  7. 07

    Month view

    Month view lists every entry for one month. Step back and forward with the arrows, filter to one property, and tap an entry to edit it or see its receipt.

  8. 08

    Schedule E

    At tax time, open Taxes. Ingot totals each property on the Schedule E lines, from rents received to total expenses and income or loss. Download it as a CSV or a PDF for your tax preparer. It's not tax advice, so have your preparer confirm the numbers.

  9. 09

    Maintenance log

    The maintenance log keeps a record of every repair: the vendor, the cost and notes. Leave the box ticked to log the cost as a repairs expense too. Untick it if you already logged the payment, like this one.

  10. 10

    Bank import

    Already have it all in your bank account? Download the transactions as a CSV and import them here. Map the columns once, tag each row to a property and category, and Ingot remembers your rules. Rows you've imported before are flagged, so nothing counts twice.

  11. 11

    Free and Pro

    The free version tracks one property for the current year, with quick add, the dashboard totals, month view, and your Schedule E totals on screen. Pro adds unlimited properties, the charts and property table, receipt photos, past years, Schedule E downloads, the maintenance log, and bank imports.
