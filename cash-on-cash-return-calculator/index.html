What cash-on-cash return means

It's the yearly cash flow divided by the cash you put into the deal. That's it. If you put in $70,000 and the place clears $4,200 a year after every bill and the mortgage, your cash-on-cash return is 6%.

It answers one question. What is my own money earning right now? It ignores appreciation and the loan getting paid down. Those matter too. They just aren't cash in your pocket this year.

The formula

Cash-on-cash return = yearly cash flow ÷ total cash invested

Yearly cash flow is rent, minus vacancy, minus every expense, minus the mortgage. Cash invested is your down payment plus closing costs plus any repairs you pay for up front.

What's a good number?

Plenty of investors aim for 8 to 12%. Some markets never get there, and people buy anyway for appreciation. A negative number means the property costs you money every month. Know that before you sign.

Be honest with the inputs. Most bad deals look good because someone left out vacancy, repairs or big-ticket replacements. EquityFoundry sets money aside for all three by default.

Cash-on-cash vs. cap rate

Cap rate ignores the loan. It's the yearly profit before the mortgage divided by the price. Use it to compare properties to each other. Cash-on-cash includes your loan, so it tells you how your deal works with your financing.
